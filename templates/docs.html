Documentation

The seven things worth understanding
before you apply.

Your approval model reads six inputs, but the terms behind them rarely get explained anywhere. This page is that explanation — what each term means, how lenders actually use it, and what you can do about it.

01

What is a credit card

A card that lets you borrow a lender's money for everyday spending, and repay it later.

Every credit card comes with a limit — the most the bank will lend you at once — and a billing cycle that resets every month. Spend during the cycle, and the bank settles the bill with the merchant immediately. You then owe that amount back, either in full by the due date or gradually, with interest, if you carry a balance.

It's revolving credit: the moment you repay, that credit line opens back up. This is different from a loan, where you borrow a fixed sum once and repay it down to zero.

02

CIBIL score

A three-digit number from 300–900 that summarises how reliably you've repaid debt in the past.

It's maintained by TransUnion CIBIL, one of India's four credit bureaus, and built from your loan and card repayment history — on-time payments, missed ones, how much of your limit you use, and how many new accounts you've opened recently. Lenders pull it the moment you apply, which is why an application itself can nudge the score slightly through what's called a hard enquiry.

03

FOIR score

Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio — the share of your monthly income already promised to other debts.

Lenders total up your existing EMIs, rent, and other fixed repayments, then check how much of your salary that eats up before a new card or loan even enters the picture. A low FOIR means more breathing room in your income; a high one signals you're already stretched.

04

Credit limit & utilisation

Your limit is the ceiling you can borrow against; utilisation is how much of it you're actually using.

A ₹20,000 balance on a ₹1,00,000 limit is 20% utilisation. Lenders and the CIBIL model both read high utilisation as a sign of dependence on credit, even if every bill gets paid on time. Staying under roughly 30% of any limit, on any single card, tends to protect your score better than paying in full each month while running close to the edge.

05

Interest & the interest-free period

Most cards charge nothing if you clear the full statement by the due date — that gap is the interest-free period.

It usually runs 18–50 days depending on when in the billing cycle you spent. Pay less than the full amount, and the interest-free period disappears entirely for that cycle — interest is then charged from the original transaction date, not just on the unpaid balance. Card interest rates in India commonly sit between 30–45% annually, which is why carrying a balance is one of the more expensive ways to borrow.

06

Billing cycle & due date

The billing cycle is the ~30-day window your spending is grouped into; the due date is when that bill is payable.

At the end of the cycle, the bank issues a statement listing everything you spent and the total owed. The due date typically falls 15–20 days after that statement date. Missing it doesn't just add interest — a payment more than 30 days late is usually what gets reported to CIBIL as a default, which is what actually damages the score.

07

Raising your odds

Small, boring habits move these numbers more reliably than any single big decision.

  • Pay the full statement amount, not just the minimum due.
  • Keep utilisation under 30% of your limit on every card, not just on average.
  • Space out new applications — each hard enquiry has a small, temporary cost.
  • Keep old accounts open — a longer history helps more than a clean slate.
  • Check your CIBIL report yearly — bureau errors are common and are free to dispute.
